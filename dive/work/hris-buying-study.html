HRIS Buying Study

Where a free trial fits in how people buy HR software, from 12 interviews with recent buyers.

Problem

Our trial was doing three jobs at once. Marketing used it as an early touch point, sales used it to close, and we had put a checkout inside it.

I wanted to know where a trial sits in a buyer’s process, and whether ours was in the right place.

Constraints

I started the study without a mandate, so it had to be quick and worth people’s time. I ran it alone in about two weeks.

I talked to people who had recently bought HR software, some of whom had shortlisted BambooHR, to learn how buying works in general, beyond our own funnel.

Decision

I ran 12 moderated interviews and pulled five findings from them.

  1. 01Whether a buyer wants a salesperson depends on how much risk they feel, more than on company size.
  2. 02First-time buyers feel the most fear, and the buying process has to calm it.
  3. 03We built the trial as an early preview but presented it as late-stage proof.
  4. 04Buyers trusted products that were honest about their limits.
  5. 05Buyers expect a trial to hold their own data. Ours comes filled with sample data.

I delivered it as a coded, interactive deck, so all six buying stages sit on one page instead of six slides.

Outcome

After the study, we changed the direction of the trial and its agent: calm the fear, get people to what they came to see quickly, and stop repeating their pain points back to them.

My manager used the research in mapping for the wider growth portfolio, so it reached teams beyond mine.

Reflection

I wish I’d run it the week I joined. Knowing where my part of the product sat in the whole buying process changed how I designed, and it helped the people around me too.
